How the stud count works

A wall is more than the studs you see in a row. This calculator follows a standard IRC-style takeoff, so the number you get is the number you actually buy, not just the field studs.

Field studs

One stud at the start of the wall, then one at every on-center mark along its length.

Plates

A single bottom plate plus a double top plate, cut from stock the height of the wall.

Corner studs

Two extra studs at each corner for strength, drywall backing and a nailing surface.

Opening studs

Four studs for every door and window: a king and a jack stud on each side of the opening.

Waste allowance

A percentage on top for culls, split ends and cutting mistakes. Ten percent suits most straight walls.

Spacing matters too: the same 16 ft wall needs 13 field studs at 16 in on-center but only 9 at 24 in on-center. Wider spacing saves lumber, yet 16 in stays the standard for load-bearing and exterior walls because it carries loads safely and backs drywall properly.

Common questions

How many studs do I need for a 16 ft wall?

At 16 in on-center spacing with no openings, a 16 ft wall needs 26 studs including plates, corners and 10 percent waste. Add a door and a window and the same wall needs 35 studs, which is the default example in the calculator above.

Should I frame at 16 in or 24 in on-center?

16 in on-center is the standard for load-bearing and exterior walls because it carries roof and floor loads safely and gives drywall solid backing. 24 in on-center is allowed for some single-story and non-load-bearing walls and uses fewer studs, but always check your local code before choosing it.

Why does the count include a double top plate?

A double top plate ties intersecting walls together, spreads loads from joists and rafters that land between studs, and gives a continuous nailing surface. The calculator counts three plate pieces per wall run: one bottom plate and two top plates.

How much waste should I add to a stud order?

Ten percent is a safe default for straight walls. It covers culls, split ends and cutting mistakes. Go up to 15 percent for walls with several openings or if your lumber yard tends to send warped stock.
